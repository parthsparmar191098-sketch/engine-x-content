A manual gearbox grinds when the parts being joined are turning at different speeds. If only one gear crunches, that gear's synchro ring is usually worn. If every gear crunches, the clutch is probably not releasing fully. Low or wrong gear oil makes both worse and quickly wears the gearbox out.

## Why does a gear change need a synchro?

Because the gears are always in mesh but turn at different speeds. In a modern gearbox every gear pair stays meshed; one gear of each pair spins freely on its shaft until a synchroniser locks it on ([Edumech](https://www.edumech.co.uk/learn/transmission-and-driveline/synchromesh-manual-transmission)). Before you select a gear, that free gear and the shaft are almost never at the same speed.

The synchroniser fixes that in a fraction of a second ([ASE study guide](https://freeasestudyguides.com/manual-transmission-synchronizers.html)):

1. The shift fork pushes the **sleeve** towards the gear, and the sleeve pushes the **blocker ring**.
2. The blocker ring's cone rubs on a cone on the gear. Friction speeds the gear up or slows it down to match the shaft.
3. Until the speeds match, the ring's pointed teeth hold the sleeve back.
4. Once they match, the sleeve slides over the gear's **dog teeth** and locks it to the shaft.

A grind is the sound of the sleeve's teeth hitting the dog teeth while the two are still turning at different speeds.

## Cause 1: a worn synchro ring

Synchro rings work by friction, so they are wear parts. Rushed shifts, skipped fluid changes and hard use wear them faster ([Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2213891/signs-synchronizers-failing-manual-transmission/)). As the cone wears, the ring can no longer match the speeds in time and the sleeve arrives too early.

The signs build up slowly. It starts as a slight notchiness or a light click in the lever, then turns into stiff shifts you have to push through, and finally a grind you can feel in your hand. The **2nd gear synchro often fails first**, because the lower gears handle bigger and more frequent speed changes ([Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2213891/signs-synchronizers-failing-manual-transmission/)).

A simple check: compare upshifts and downshifts. Upshifts are easier, because the falling engine speed helps the synchro. Downshifts make it speed up the clutch disc and input shaft, so if upshifts are quiet but downshifts crunch or hesitate, the synchros are the likely cause.

## Cause 2: a clutch that does not fully release

If **every** gear grinds, look at the clutch first ([AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm)). When the pedal is down, the clutch should stop the engine driving the input shaft. If it drags, the input shaft keeps being driven while you shift, and no synchro can win that fight.

A dragging clutch can also make shifting stiff and so look like synchro wear ([Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2213891/signs-synchronizers-failing-manual-transmission/)). Causes include a leaking clutch master or slave cylinder, a stretched or misadjusted cable and a worn release mechanism ([AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm)). These are usually cheaper to fix than the gearbox, so rule them out first.

## Cause 3: low or wrong gear oil

The synchros, bearings and gears all live on splash lubrication. **Low fluid can destroy a manual transmission within a few thousand miles** ([AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm)). Oil that is too thick for the weather makes shifting sluggish when cold, and oil that is too thin can raise noise and wear when hot. Factory fills vary from gear oil to automatic transmission fluid or even engine oil, so use what your car's maker specifies ([AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm)).

## What the symptom tells you

| Symptom | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| One gear grinds | That gear's synchro ring is worn |
| Every gear grinds | Clutch dragging, not fully releasing |
| Downshifts crunch, upshifts are fine | Worn synchros |
| Stiff or notchy when cold | Gear oil too thick for the temperature |
| Noise in every gear at all speeds | Worn shaft bearings or low oil |
| Pops out of gear under load | Worn synchro, dog teeth, fork, rail or detent |

The table is drawn from [AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm) and [Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2213891/signs-synchronizers-failing-manual-transmission/).

## Why does a car pop out of gear?

Because the sleeve is not staying fully engaged on the dog teeth. The teeth are normally given a slight reverse taper, so load pulls the sleeve tighter into gear; a worn synchro or worn teeth lose that grip ([Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2213891/signs-synchronizers-failing-manual-transmission/)). Bent or loose shift forks and rails, worn hub splines, missing interlock parts, too much shaft end play and even a broken engine mount can do the same ([AA1Car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/2004/ic20428.htm)).

Reverse is a special case: it is usually straight-cut and not synchronised, so it is meant to be selected only with the car stopped ([Edumech](https://www.edumech.co.uk/learn/transmission-and-driveline/synchromesh-manual-transmission)). A crunch into reverse while rolling is normal behaviour, not a fault, but it still chips the teeth.

## The engineering takeaway

A synchro is a small cone clutch whose only job is to match two speeds before the dog teeth meet. When it wears, one gear crunches; when the clutch drags, all of them do; and without the right oil, everything wears faster. Check the oil and the clutch first, because they are the cheap fixes.
